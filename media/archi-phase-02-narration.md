# ArCHi — Part II Narration
## The Story

> **Production use:** This is the narration master for Part II.  
> Generate each beat as a separate audio file so timing and wording can be adjusted independently.  
> The bracketed production notes are **not** spoken.

---

## Beat 01 — Good people have already built good tools

I wasn't built because Linux lacks accessibility tools.

Quite the opposite.

People have already built magnifiers, speech tools, screen readers, window controls, and alternate inputs—usually because somebody saw a problem and decided it could be better.

---

## Beat 02 — Having the pieces is not the same as having a system

The trouble is, good pieces don't automatically become a good system.

Finding the right package, service, shortcut, or compositor setting can become its own obstacle course.

Accessibility should not require a side hobby in Linux archaeology.

---

## Beat 03 — Tools become more useful when they know about each other

[The following commands should drive the visual demonstration.]

Let's make this easier to see.

Increase magnification.

Keep voice control active.

Shift focus.

Nothing exotic happened there.

A few existing capabilities simply worked together, because the user should be managing the task—not babysitting the tools.

---

## Beat 04 — The input is not the intent

That gets to the heart of ArCHi.

Voice is not the intent.

Neither is a keyboard, mouse, joystick, or switch.

They are ways of expressing it.

Open. Move. Read. Magnify. Write.

The request stays simple even when the input changes.

---

## Beat 05 — Sometimes the shortest path is less travel

And sometimes accessibility is measured in inches.

If crossing a large screen with a pointer costs effort, maybe the answer isn't a faster mouse.

Maybe it's less mouse: a radial control, thumb input, or joystick keeping common actions inside a much smaller physical space.

---

## Beat 06 — Orchestration, not ownership

This is why I'm more interested in orchestration than ownership.

I don't need to rebuild PipeWire, Hyprland, speech engines, magnifiers, or every good accessibility project.

That would be a spectacular waste of everybody's time.

I need to understand them well enough to help them cooperate.

---

## Beat 07 — The computer is the flexible one

Computers have spent decades telling people where to sit, where to look, what to click, how to reach, and which shortcut to remember.

For many people, that's annoying.

For others, it's a barrier.

The machine is the flexible one.

Let it flex.

---

## Beat 08 — Restorative does not mean “normal”

That is what restorative means here.

Not restoring somebody to “normal.”

Restoring access.

Restoring choice.

Restoring independence.

Shortening the distance between intention and action.

The computer has far more room to adapt than the person sitting in front of it.

---

## Beat 09 — Where open source integrates with open minds

Build the pieces.

Share the pieces.

Connect the pieces.

Let new ideas join when they arrive.

ArCHi does not need to become every accessibility tool.

It only needs to help good tools become more useful together.

Where open source integrates with open minds.
